## Interview Process Overview

> Note: This is for the _intern_ interview process.

### Application + Direct Consideration

Google internship roles typically open up in late summer to Fall for the following summer. The application process is competitive, and Google receives a large number of applications for their internship positions. I am not exactly sure what they look for, as I got ghosted the first two years I applied, but managed

To get the process started, you'll need to apply on Google's Careers website. Note that there are typically two separate paths - "direct consideration", and the regular process. From what we understand, Google marks some candidates (whether from previous cycles, recruiting events, etc.) as "direct consideration" candidates, which means, if you go through with it, Google will open your application for you. If you do get this process, it'll typically come through email, and you'll get information on what it means. There are plenty, if not the majority of interns who do not get direct consideration, and go through the regular process, so don't be worried if you don't get this.

---

## 1. Initial Recruiter Outreach/Online Assessment

It seems like if you pass an initial resume screen, the process starts with a recruiter email asking if you’re interested in moving forward, or they'll just send a link to the online assessment.

- Format: 2 coding questions
  - Duration: 90 minutes
  - This is done on Google's internal platform
  - Typical DS&A leetcode-style
  - No test cases are provided - you'll have a sample test case or two, but unlike most popular OAs like CodeSignal, you won't have a comprehensive suite to test correctness
    - Thus, you **should** write your own test cases and run against them

After that, if you pass, you'll move to the interview stage.

## Interview(s)

### 2. Interview Structure

You'll get an email from a recruiter asking if you're interested in moving on! They may ask some basic questions (e.g. timezone, degree, graduation date, updated resume + transcripts), as well as asking to provide your availability. Should you proceed, you'll have

| Role   | Rounds                           |
| ------ | -------------------------------- |
| Intern | 2 (maybe 3) Technical Interviews |

- Length: 45 minutes
- Format: Typically 1 coding question
  - These can be standard LC or more open-ended, no code running
- Typical Leetcode-style interview expectations:
  - Ask clarifying questions at the start
  - Talk through your approach out loud
  - Write clean, optimal code
  - Handle edge cases
  - Discuss time & space complexity
  - Note that these questions may be more open-ended than the typical Leetcode questions like Meta or Amazon may be
    - Follow-ups are also something that you should expect from Google, and not necessarily just a "solve the question" and be done
    - They may ask you to optimize your solution, remove some constraints, add some, or ask about tradeoffs compared to other approaches
    - We've somewhat seen an emphasis on trees/graph problems, but this is not necessarily the case - best thing to do is still prepare!

Note that we wrote _maybe_ 3 as sometimes your interviews don't provide enough signal for the hiring team to make a decision, and they may ask for a 3rd interview. This does not mean you're not going to get an offer! We know multiple people who have done these additional interviews and gone on to team matching + offers.

## 3. Team Matching + Offer Timeline

Typically, the recruiter will come back to you after your interviews to let you know if you passed or not. This may take anywhere from 2 weeks to a month or even more.

If you did, typically you'll move on to team matching, which is a process where you'll be matched with teams that are looking for interns. For the 2027 cycle, it seems like Google may be trying something new where they give candidates a fungible offer after passing the interviews. This means you are guaranteed an offer, but elect to be open to all locations and teams within Google. This is a new process, and we don't know how it will work out, but it seems like a good way to get more candidates into the process.

Team matching has historically started in late November (think around Thanksgiving) or early December, and can take weeks to months to complete depending on how teams are looking for interns, which types of teams are looking, etc. Don't necessarily expect an offer or team if you've made it to this stage, but be pretty proud regardless! This is a huge accomplishment, and at this point, it really turns to whatever
